Observing windows

  • Airmass: Kasten & Young (1989), normalized so X(90°) = 1; curves are hidden below the horizon and the dotted line marks X = 2.
  • Twilight shading marks day / civil / nautical / astro / night.
  • Entered ICRS coordinates are precessed to the mean equator/equinox of date (IAU 2006; nutation omitted), so altitudes agree with the desktop app to ~0.01°. The Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet; mirrors tried in order).
  • Chart navigation: the wheel pans the time window, Ctrl/⌘+wheel zooms around the cursor, dragging the left or right plot edge resizes the window, dragging inside pans, and double-clicking resets to the full range. Hovering the plot shows each target’s altitude, azimuth, airmass and parallactic angle at that instant.
  • Initial workspace generated by airmass-gui on 2026-11-21T14:33:28; edits stay in this browser (localStorage) until you export or reset.
  • Sharing: the URL encodes the full workspace (#w= fragment) — Copy link in the Workspace panel copies it, and opening it loads that exact setup.
  • Workspaces: save named copies in this browser, import an exported JSON file, or download the chart as a PNG image (Export JSON keeps the desktop-app file shape).
  • Windows: the dashed line marks your airmass threshold; shaded bands show when each target is usable, colored ticks on the time axis mark transits, and the panel below the chart lists per-night windows, transit times and airmass.
  • Moon: with Moon checked, a dashed curve shows the Moon’s airmass and the tooltip adds its altitude, illumination fraction, and angular separation from each target.
  • Touch and keys: a single finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom, and a tap shows the tooltip; the arrow keys pan, + / - zoom around the center, and 0 or r resets the window. With Civil / Nautical twilight checked, thin vertical lines mark the sun’s -6° and -12° crossings.
