Instructions
  • Observatory: pick a preset or enter latitude, longitude, and elevation; the time zone dropdown lists common zones (the current zone is preselected).
  • Time range: the start date in local time and the number of days shown; the Today button centers the window on the current night; the 1 / 2 / 3 buttons set the duration to one, two, or three days.
  • Display: curve mode (airmass, altitude, or azimuth); Windows adds the airmass threshold, the shaded usable bands, and the per-night list; Moon, the twilight overlays, Weather, and Extra info toggle their features.
  • Targets: one row per target with its color, name, RA, Dec, and V magnitude; the Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet), + adds a row, and × removes it.
  • Main chart: left axis airmass, right axis altitude; bottom axis local time, top UTC. Shading marks day, twilight, and night; the red circle at the top marks the current time. Hover for each target's details. The wheel scrolls the page; Ctrl/⌘+wheel zooms about the cursor; dragging inside the plot pans; the + / − buttons, the scrollbar, and the arrow keys adjust the view; 0, R, or a double-click resets. A rain drop or a lightning bolt along the top marks forecast hours with rain or thunder, and a moon glyph with its illumination percent marks each local midnight.
  • Observing windows: per night, the usable windows with durations, transit times and airmass, the field (parallactic-angle) rotation accrued above the airmass threshold, and the Moon's phase at mid-night.
  • Annual calendar: twelve months of coverage per target — usable hours, usable nights, hours per night, the span of usable days, and the month's best airmass.
  • Workspace: save named copies in this browser, import an exported JSON file, export, Copy link (encodes the full setup in the URL), download the chart as a PNG, and reset to the initial state.
  • Theme: Dark, Light, or Red for night observing; the choice is remembered in this browser.
Extra details
  • Airmass: Kasten & Young (1989), normalized so X(90°) = 1; the airmass and altitude curves are hidden below the horizon, and in airmass mode the dotted line marks X = 2.
  • Twilight shading marks day / civil / nautical / astro / night.
  • Entered ICRS coordinates are precessed to the mean equator/equinox of date (IAU 2006; nutation omitted), so altitudes agree with the desktop app to ~0.01°. The Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet; mirrors tried in order).
  • Chart navigation: the wheel scrolls the page as usual, Ctrl/⌘+wheel anywhere over the chart zooms about the cursor, and the + / − buttons zoom, the scrollbar under the chart pans (drag the thumb) or jumps (click the track), dragging inside the plot pans, dragging the left or right plot edge resizes the window, dragging the chart’s right edge, bottom edge, or bottom-right corner resizes it (the same edges resize any panel), dragging the strip at the right edge of the left column resizes the whole column, and double-clicking or the ↺ button resets to the full range; the arrow keys pan by 10% of the visible window, + / − zoom, and 0 or R resets. The bottom time axis is local, the top one UTC; hovering shows both times plus each target’s altitude, azimuth, airmass, parallactic angle, and its rotation rate in degrees per minute at that instant.
  • Initial workspace generated by airmass-gui on 2026-11-24T23:09:40; edits stay in this browser (localStorage) until you export or reset.
  • Sharing: the URL encodes the full workspace (#w= fragment) — Copy link in the Workspace panel copies it, and opening it loads that exact setup.
  • Workspaces: save named copies in this browser, import an exported JSON file, or download the chart as a PNG image (Export JSON keeps the desktop-app file shape).
  • Now: when the current time falls in the displayed window, a red vertical line with a filled circle at the top of the chart marks it.
  • Windows: the dashed line marks your airmass threshold; shaded bands show when each target is usable, colored ticks on the time axis mark transits, and the panel below the chart lists per-night windows, transit times and airmass, plus the total field (parallactic-angle) rotation accrued while the target is below the threshold, and the Moon's phase (illumination and name) at mid-night.
  • Annual calendar: per target, twelve months of coverage — usable hours, usable nights (distinct days with at least one usable hour), usable hours per night, the span of usable days, and the local time and airmass of the month’s lowest-airmass sample above the horizon. An hour is usable while the target is below the threshold and the sky is dark (sun at or below −12°); sampling is one hour, so windows shorter than about an hour may be missed.
  • Weather (hidden by default; enable it with the Weather checkbox in the Display panel): the cloud band fills the plot with the hourly cloud cover; a rain drop along the top marks hours with precipitation or at least a 40% rain chance and a lightning bolt marks thunderstorm hours (WMO weather codes 95/96/99); each per-night list adds the average clear-sky fraction. The forecast runs from about 16 days back to about 15 days ahead (needs internet).
  • Moon symbols: a glyph at each local midnight shows the Moon’s phase, with its illumination in percent beside it; always shown, independent of the Moon curve.
  • Targets: with Extra info checked, a Target details window under the rows shows each target's full Simbad record (magnitudes in all bands, spectral type, parallax with its uncertainty and the inverted distance, proper motion, radial velocity, and identifiers); a row's status text shows the stored ephemeris point count, and clicking its cross clears the record and ephemeris.
  • Finding chart (hidden by default; enable it with the Finding chart checkbox in the Display panel): a circular star chart centered on the selected target, drawn from a Simbad coordinate query (needs internet; nearby objects sized by V magnitude, with an equatorial grid, N / E arrows, north up, east left, and a cross on each other workspace target), plus a link that opens the SkyView quick-query page for a real sky image of the same field in the selected survey (DSS, HEAO 1, RASS, TESS, IRAS 100μm, or 0408 MHz; needs internet; opens in a new tab); the chart is drawn in J2000 while the image is centered on the target’s equinox-of-date position, so the two agree to about a quarter of a degree; hovering the star chart shows the equatorial coordinates under the cursor and, for the nearest Simbad object, its name, angular offset from the cursor, and a detail table (RA/Dec, magnitudes in all available bands, spectral type, object type, offset from the target, and bibliography/note counts); a second Simbad lookup by name adds the object's parallax with uncertainty, distance, proper motion, radial velocity, and identifiers to the table (cached per name, throttled to one query per 1.5 s, retried after about 10 minutes on failure); the zoom slider (logarithmic) runs the search radius from 0.5′ to 5°, with the current radius shown beside it, and the chart re-scales live as the slider moves.
  • Moon: with Moon checked, a dashed curve shows the Moon’s airmass and the tooltip adds its altitude, illumination fraction, phase name, and angular separation from each target.
  • Touch and keys: a single finger pans (on the right edge, bottom edge, or corner of the chart or any panel it resizes that window instead; on the sidebar strip it resizes the left column), two fingers pinch to zoom, and a tap shows the tooltip; the arrow keys pan, + / - zoom around the center, and 0 or r resets the window. With Civil / Nautical twilight checked, thin vertical lines mark the sun’s -6° and -12° crossings.
