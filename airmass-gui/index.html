Extra details
  • Airmass: Kasten & Young (1989), normalized so X(90°) = 1; curves are hidden below the horizon and the dotted line marks X = 2.
  • Twilight shading marks day / civil / nautical / astro / night.
  • Entered ICRS coordinates are precessed to the mean equator/equinox of date (IAU 2006; nutation omitted), so altitudes agree with the desktop app to ~0.01°. The Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet; mirrors tried in order).
  • Chart navigation: the wheel pans the time window, Ctrl/⌘+wheel or the + / − buttons zoom, the scrollbar under the chart pans (drag the thumb) or jumps (click the track), dragging inside the plot pans, dragging the left or right plot edge resizes the window, and double-clicking or the ↺ button resets to the full range; the arrow keys pan by 10% of the visible window, + / − zoom, and 0 or R resets. The bottom time axis is local, the top one UTC; hovering shows both times plus each target’s altitude, azimuth, airmass and parallactic angle at that instant.
  • Initial workspace generated by airmass-gui on 2026-11-21T19:10:34; edits stay in this browser (localStorage) until you export or reset.
  • Sharing: the URL encodes the full workspace (#w= fragment) — Copy link in the Workspace panel copies it, and opening it loads that exact setup.
  • Workspaces: save named copies in this browser, import an exported JSON file, or download the chart as a PNG image (Export JSON keeps the desktop-app file shape).
  • Windows: the dashed line marks your airmass threshold; shaded bands show when each target is usable, colored ticks on the time axis mark transits, and the panel below the chart lists per-night windows, transit times and airmass, plus the total field (parallactic-angle) rotation accrued while the target is below the threshold.
  • S/N (panel hidden by default; enable it with the S/N estimate checkbox in the Options panel): with the panel on, each per-night window also lists, at the window midpoint, the single exposure that reaches the target S/N (or the S/N of the fixed exposure), from a V-band model: an 8.75×10³ e⁻¹ cm⁻² s⁻¹ zero point, 21.9 mag arcsec⁻² of dark sky, a Moon term scaled by phase and altitude (Houdashelt et al. 2005), and 0.10 mag per airmass of extinction. The V magnitude is the target’s (Simbad lookup or the V mag column); the defaults are editable in the S/N panel.
  • Moon: with Moon checked, a dashed curve shows the Moon’s airmass and the tooltip adds its altitude, illumination fraction, and angular separation from each target.
  • Touch and keys: a single finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom, and a tap shows the tooltip; the arrow keys pan, + / - zoom around the center, and 0 or r resets the window. With Civil / Nautical twilight checked, thin vertical lines mark the sun’s -6° and -12° crossings.
