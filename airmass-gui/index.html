Instructions
  • Observatory: pick a preset or enter latitude, longitude, and elevation; the time zone dropdown lists common zones (the current zone is preselected).
  • Time range: the start date in local time and the number of days shown; the Today button centers the window on the current night; the 1 / 2 / 3 buttons set the duration to one, two, or three days; the Night of dropdown jumps the range to any local night from yesterday through two weeks ahead (each entry gives the night's best target altitude and the Moon's illumination at its closing midnight).
  • Display: curve mode (airmass, altitude, or azimuth); Windows adds the airmass threshold line, the transit ticks, and the per-night list; Usable bands toggles the shaded bands showing when each target is at or below the threshold; Moon, the twilight overlays, Weather, Weather report, and Extra info toggle their features.
  • Panel order: the small up/down arrows at the right end of each window's title move that window one slot up or down within its column (the sidebar windows among themselves, the main-column windows among themselves), skipping hidden windows; the arrows are disabled at the ends. The order is per-page only; it returns to the built-in layout on reload or Reset.
  • Targets: one row per target with its color, name, RA, Dec, and V magnitude; the name is a link to the object's CDS Simbad page (opens in a new tab; double-click renames it in place, Enter saves, Esc cancels); the Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet), + adds a row, ↻ refreshes the row's Simbad record (parallax, distance, proper motion, identifiers), and × removes it.
  • Main chart: left axis airmass, right axis altitude; bottom axis local time, top UTC. Shading marks day, twilight, and night; the red circle at the top marks the current time. Hover for each target's details. The wheel scrolls the page; Ctrl/⌘+wheel zooms about the cursor; dragging inside the plot pans; the + / − buttons, the scrollbar, and the arrow keys adjust the view; the ◎ button expands the time range until the current time sits exactly at its center, mirroring the longer of the before-now and after-now portions onto the shorter side; 0, R, or a double-click resets. A rain drop or a lightning bolt along the top marks forecast hours with rain or thunder, and a moon glyph with its illumination percent marks each local midnight.
  • Observing windows: the target name links to its CDS Simbad page (new tab); per night, the usable windows with durations, transit times and airmass, the field (parallactic-angle) rotation accrued above the airmass threshold, the Moon's phase at mid-night and its rise and set that night (or whether it stays up or down all night), and, with Weather on, the day's maximum hourly rain chance and average clear-sky fraction.
  • Annual calendar: the target name links to its CDS Simbad page (new tab); twelve months of coverage per target — usable hours, usable nights, hours per night, the span of usable days, and the month's best airmass.
  • Workspace: save named copies in this browser (each dropdown label shows the saved setup’s name, observatory, target count, and save time in UTC), import an exported JSON file, export, Copy link (encodes the full setup in the URL), download the chart as a PNG, and reset to the initial state.
  • Theme: Dark, Light, or Red for night observing; the choice is remembered in this browser.
Extra details
  • Airmass: Kasten & Young (1989), normalized so X(90°) = 1; the airmass and altitude curves are hidden below the horizon, and in airmass mode the dotted line marks X = 2.
  • Twilight shading marks day / civil / nautical / astro / night.
  • Entered ICRS coordinates are precessed to the mean equator/equinox of date (IAU 2006; nutation omitted), so altitudes agree with the desktop app to ~0.01°. The Simbad button looks a name up in CDS Simbad (needs internet; mirrors tried in order).
  • Chart navigation: the wheel scrolls the page as usual, Ctrl/⌘+wheel anywhere over the chart zooms about the cursor, and the + / − buttons zoom, the scrollbar under the chart pans (drag the thumb) or jumps (click the track), dragging inside the plot pans, dragging the left or right plot edge resizes the window, dragging the chart’s right edge, bottom edge, or bottom-right corner resizes it (the same edges resize any panel), dragging the strip at the right edge of the left column resizes the whole column, and double-clicking or the ↺ button resets to the full range; the ◎ button expands the time range symmetrically about the current time: the longer of the before-now and after-now portions is kept and mirrored onto the shorter side, so the current time sits exactly at the middle of the range (when the current time is outside the range, the range grows until it is contained), and the zoomed view resets to the full range; the arrow keys pan by 10% of the visible window, + / − zoom, and 0 or R resets. The bottom time axis is local, the top one UTC; hovering shows both times plus each target’s altitude, azimuth, airmass, parallactic angle, and its rotation rate in degrees per minute at that instant.
  • Panel order: the up/down arrows at the right end of each window's title move that window one visible slot up or down within its column (the sidebar windows among themselves, the main-column windows among themselves), skipping hidden windows; the arrows are disabled at the column ends. The order is per-page only and is not saved with the state.
  • Initial workspace (site/default_workspace.json) generated by airmass-gui on 2026-11-28T08:27:50; a first visit and Reset center its range on tonight's local midnight, keeping its duration. Edits stay in this browser (localStorage) until you export or reset.
  • Night of: the dropdown in the Time range panel offers the local nights from yesterday through fourteen days ahead, each labeled with the best altitude of up to two targets over that night (sampled every half hour) and the Moon's illumination at the night's closing midnight; choosing an entry sets the range to that local midnight for one day, and any range that does not start on an offered night shows as (custom range).
  • Sharing: the URL encodes the full workspace (#w= fragment) — Copy link in the Workspace panel copies it, and opening it loads that exact setup.
  • Workspaces: save named copies in this browser (stored as {state, saved}; copies saved by older versions without a save time keep their name only as the label and still load), import an exported JSON file, or download the chart as a PNG image (Export JSON keeps the desktop-app file shape).
  • Now: when the current time falls in the displayed window, a red vertical line with a filled circle at the top of the chart marks it.
  • Windows: the dashed line marks your airmass threshold, colored ticks on the time axis mark transits, and the panel below the chart lists per-night windows, transit times and airmass, plus the total field (parallactic-angle) rotation accrued while the target is below the threshold, the Moon's phase (illumination and name) at mid-night, that night's Moon rise and set in local time (or a note that the Moon stays up or down for the whole day's part), and, with Weather on, the day's maximum hourly rain chance and average clear-sky fraction.
  • Annual calendar: per target, twelve months of coverage — usable hours, usable nights (distinct days with at least one usable hour), usable hours per night, the span of usable days, and the local time and airmass of the month’s lowest-airmass sample above the horizon. An hour is usable while the target is below the threshold and the sky is dark (sun at or below −12°); sampling is one hour, so windows shorter than about an hour may be missed.
  • Weather (hidden by default; enable it with the Weather checkbox in the Display panel): the cloud band fills the plot with the hourly cloud cover; a rain drop along the top marks hours with precipitation or at least a 40% rain chance and a lightning bolt marks thunderstorm hours (WMO weather codes 95/96/99); each per-night list adds the day's maximum hourly rain chance and the average clear-sky fraction. The forecast runs from about 16 days back to about 15 days ahead (needs internet).
  • Weather report (hidden by default; enable it with the Weather report checkbox in the Display panel): the current conditions at the observatory, then one row per day for eight days \u2014 condition and its display symbol, min and max temperature, maximum rain chance, mean cloud cover, maximum wind, and the Moon's illumination at the day's closing midnight \u2014 with links to the underlying weather services, including the Meteoblue meteogram for the site's coordinates (needs internet; fetched from open-meteo and cached for one hour; the links open in new tabs).
  • Moon symbols: a glyph at each local midnight shows the Moon’s phase, with its illumination in percent beside it; always shown, independent of the Moon curve.
  • Targets: with Extra info checked, a Target details window under the rows shows each target's full Simbad record (magnitudes in all bands, spectral type, parallax with its uncertainty and the inverted distance, proper motion, radial velocity, and identifiers); a row's status text shows the stored ephemeris point count, and clicking its cross clears the record and ephemeris.
  • Finding chart (hidden by default; enable it with the Finding chart checkbox in the Display panel): a star chart centered on the selected target, drawn from a Simbad coordinate query (needs internet; nearby objects sized by V magnitude, N / E arrows, north up, east left, and a cross on each other workspace target, names to the left of the crosses), plus an inline sky image: a DSS2 color cutout generated by the CDS HiPS2FITS service (up to 15′ across at about 1.8″ per pixel; needs internet), a scale bar below it, and a note that the image is unavailable if the service does not reply with one; the chart and the image are centered on the target’s J2000 position, so they agree, and the cutout’s field of view is twice the chart’s radius, so the two cover the same field; the chart and the image are the same fixed size, and each window grows taller, not wider, as its info tables fill in; hovering the star chart or the sky image shows the equatorial coordinates under the cursor and, for the nearest Simbad object drawn in the pane being hovered (the star query reaches past both panes’ fields to the canvas diagonal), its name and angular offset from the cursor (the nearest object is marked with an encircled crosshair on whichever of the star chart and the sky image shows it, in the theme’s highlight color: red in dark and light, white in red/night), with its basic fields below the star chart (RA/Dec, magnitudes in all available bands, spectral type, object type, offset from the target, and bibliography/note counts) and its record fields below the sky image (parallax with uncertainty, distance, proper motion, radial velocity, and identifiers), a dash in a row the object lacks; the record fields come from a second Simbad lookup by name, shown as a "querying" row while in flight (cached per name, throttled to one query per 1.5 s, retried after about 10 minutes on failure);; the star chart’s caption links the selected target to its CDS Simbad name-search page (opens in a new tab), clicking the encircled crosshair on the star chart or the sky image opens the hovered object’s page (new tab), and target names in the Targets panel open the same pages; the star query pads the radius out to the square canvas’s diagonal, so the whole canvas is covered; the zoom slider (logarithmic) runs the search radius from 0.5′ to 7.5′, the sky image’s maximum field (15′ across), with the current radius shown beside it, the chart re-scaling live as the slider moves and the sky image re-fetching to match.
  • Moon: with Moon checked, a dashed curve shows the Moon’s airmass as seen from the observatory (topocentric position, geocentric position plus diurnal parallax) and the tooltip adds its altitude, illumination fraction, phase name, and angular separation from each target.
  • Touch and keys: a single finger pans (on the right edge, bottom edge, or corner of the chart or any panel it resizes that window instead; on the sidebar strip it resizes the left column), two fingers pinch to zoom, and a tap shows the tooltip; the arrow keys pan, + / - zoom around the center, and 0 or r resets the window. With Civil / Nautical twilight checked, thin vertical lines mark the sun’s -6° and -12° crossings.
